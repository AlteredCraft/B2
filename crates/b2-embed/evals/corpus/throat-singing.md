title: Throat singing

Two notes at once from one voice: a low fundamental held steady in the chest
while the mouth's shape carves its overtones into a whistling melody floating
above. The khoomei tradition of the Tuvan steppe names half a dozen styles —
sygyt's bright flute-like line, kargyraa's growl an octave beneath the drone.
Learning starts with finding the buzz: hum, then slowly shift the tongue as if
gliding between vowels until a faint second tone rings out, and strengthen it
by exaggerating the jaw's position rather than pushing more air. Herders
developed the art in the open steppe, and recordings that reach Western ears
usually feature sygyt, the style easiest to pick out of an ensemble.
