## Getting licensed

The entry-level license here tests spectrum rules and operating practice,
not electronics — you don't need Ohm's law to pass, just to know which
bands you're allowed on and why you identify with your callsign every ten
minutes. A cheap dual-band handheld gets a new operator onto the local
repeater network the same day: a repeater retransmits a weak handheld
signal from a hilltop antenna, turning a two-mile line-of-sight radio into
one that reaches thirty or forty miles across a metro area. Programming one
means finding its input and output frequencies plus its sub-audible tone,
which keeps the repeater from keying up on someone else's unrelated signal
sharing the same channel. The deeper rabbit hole is the high-frequency
bands, which bounce off the upper atmosphere and can reach the other side
of the planet on a few watts — but the layer that makes it possible rises
and falls with the sun, so which band actually works changes by the hour
and the season. A contest weekend is the fastest way to learn a band's
personality: hours of fast, formulaic exchanges reveal exactly when a
frequency opens and closes far better than any propagation chart does.

## Paying it back

Sleep pressure builds the way hunger does — a homeostatic drive, not a
choice — driven mostly by a chemical accumulating in the brain across every
waking hour and clearing out during sleep. Cut two hours a night for a week
and the deficit doesn't reset with one long weekend lie-in: recovery sleep
repays the most acute costs — reaction time, mood — within a night or two,
but subtler costs to working memory and glucose regulation take longer to
clear, and a chronic short-sleeper who insists they've adapted usually
tests just as impaired as the first night; they've simply stopped noticing.
Naps repay the debt only within limits — twenty minutes taken before
mid-afternoon borrows against the pressure that would otherwise carry a
person to a normal bedtime, while a long or late nap can burn off enough of
that pressure to make falling asleep that night harder, not easier, the
same debt spent at the wrong time. None of this substitutes for not
accruing the debt in the first place; it's closer to a high-interest loan
than a savings account.
