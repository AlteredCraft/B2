Transparent pigment suspended in water, laid onto absorbent paper in thin
washes that dry lighter than they look. The white of the page is the only
white available, so highlights are planned as reserved paper rather than
added at the end, and every stroke commits — lifting dried pigment never
quite restores the surface. Working wet-into-wet lets colors feather into one
another with soft edges; letting a layer dry before glazing the next keeps
edges crisp. Cheap brushes splay and cheap paper buckles, which makes the
paper the one material worth paying for.
