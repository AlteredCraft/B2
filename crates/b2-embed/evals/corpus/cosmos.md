---
type: note
title: The observable universe
---
The observable universe is the sphere of everything whose glow has had time to
arrive: about 46 billion light-years in radius, holding on the order of two
trillion galaxies. Every distant galaxy recedes, and the farther it is, the
faster — not because things fly apart through space but because space itself
expands, stretching the wavelengths of travelling photons toward the red. The
oldest signal we can detect is the cosmic microwave background, a nearly
uniform afterglow released when the young universe first became transparent.
Beyond the horizon there is presumably more universe; it is simply not
observable, and never will be, because expansion carries it away faster than
its signals can approach.
