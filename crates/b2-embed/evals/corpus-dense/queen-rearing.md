type: note
title: Queen rearing
---
Any fertilized egg can become a queen; what decides is diet. Nurse bees flood
a chosen larva with royal jelly in an oversized vertical cell, and sixteen
days later a virgin emerges — a week faster than a worker. Small-scale queen
rearing borrows that reflex: graft day-old larvae into cell cups, place the
bar in a queenless, nurse-heavy cell-builder colony, and it will draw a row
of queen cells on demand. The virgin's mating flights in her first two weeks
fix her quality for life — she mates in the air with a dozen or more drones
from surrounding apiaries and never mates again. Requeening from your calmest,
mite-tolerant colony every year or two is the slowest, surest improvement a
small apiary can make.
