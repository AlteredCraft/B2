type: note
title: Swarm season
---
Swarming is the colony reproducing: in late spring a strong hive raises a
crop of queen cells, and the old queen leaves with half the workers and most
of the stored nectar in their stomachs, bivouacking on a branch while scouts
argue over cavities. For the beekeeper it is losing half the workforce a
month before the main nectar flow. The tells come in order — a brood nest
backfilled with nectar, congestion at the entrance, play cups along the frame
bottoms, then charged queen cups with an egg and royal jelly. Once a queen
cell is capped the colony has usually already decided; the window for
persuasion closes a week earlier than most first-year beekeepers believe.
