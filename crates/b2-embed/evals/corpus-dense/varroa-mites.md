type: note
title: Varroa mites
---
The parasite that kills more colonies than anything else: a reddish-brown mite
the size of a pinhead that rides adult bees and breeds inside capped brood
cells, feeding on developing pupae and spreading viruses as it goes. An
infestation builds invisibly through summer and collapses the colony in
autumn, so monitoring is the discipline — an alcohol wash of roughly three
hundred nurse bees gives a mite count per hundred bees, and anything over two
or three per hundred calls for intervention. A sticky board under the screened
bottom counts natural mite drop instead, cruder but bee-friendly. Counting is
not optional: a hive that looks booming in July can be a dead-out by November
on mite-vectored viruses alone.
