type: note
title: Winter prep
---
Winter survival is decided in September. Heft the hive from behind: a colony
needs the weight of roughly forty pounds of stored honey to carry it through,
and light hives get thick syrup while it is still warm enough to take it.
Fit the mouse guard before the first cold night — a mouse in a clustered hive
shreds comb all winter unopposed. Tilt the box slightly forward so
condensation runs down the front wall instead of dripping on the cluster;
top insulation under the roof matters more than wrapping the sides. Then
stop opening the hive. The bees form a tight cluster around the queen,
shivering their flight muscles for heat, and every crack of the lid costs
them stores they cannot replace until the willows bloom.
