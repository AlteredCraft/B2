---
type: note
title: Feeding bees
---
Syrup concentration is a message, not just food. Thin one-to-one sugar syrup
in spring mimics a nectar flow and tells the colony to rear brood and draw
comb; thick two-to-one syrup in autumn is winter stores, ripened and capped
fast with less work. Once the days turn cold the bees cannot dry syrup at
all, and feeding switches to fondant or dry sugar laid directly over the
cluster. A new colony on undrawn foundation gets fed continuously whatever
the season — wax costs the bees eight pounds of honey per pound built.
Feed inside the hive, never in the open: an open feeder in a dearth teaches
every colony in range to rob, starting with yours.
