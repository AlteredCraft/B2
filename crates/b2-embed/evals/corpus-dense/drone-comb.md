type: note
title: Drone comb
---
Drones — the colony's males — are reared in visibly larger cells with domed
cappings, take the longest of any caste to emerge, and do no foraging; their
one job is to mate. That long capped period is exactly what varroa prefers,
because a mite that slips into a drone cell raises more offspring before the
capping opens. Beekeepers turn the preference into a trap: hang a frame of
drone-sized foundation in the brood nest, let the queen fill it, and freeze
the frame once the drone brood is capped — mites and all — before returning
it. Cheap, chemical-free mite control, at the cost of remembering the
schedule: forget the frozen-frame rotation and the trap becomes a mite
nursery instead.
