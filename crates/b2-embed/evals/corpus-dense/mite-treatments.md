---
type: note
title: Mite treatments
---
Once a wash shows the mite count over threshold, the treatment options each
trade coverage against constraints. Oxalic acid — dribbled in syrup or
vaporized — kills phoretic mites riding adult bees but cannot reach mites
sealed in capped brood, so it shines in broodless windows: late autumn, or
after caging the queen. Formic acid penetrates cappings and treats brood too,
but is temperature-sensitive and can harm queens in a heat wave. Thymol strips
work gently over weeks and taint honey supers, so they wait until the harvest
is off. Rotating treatment families keeps resistance from building. The
calendar matters as much as the chemical: treat too late and the winter bees
were already reared damaged, whatever the count says afterwards.
